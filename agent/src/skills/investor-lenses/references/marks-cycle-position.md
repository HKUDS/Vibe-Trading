# Cycle Positioning Lens

> **Framework, not investment advice.** This is our own summary of a publicly
> documented methodology, reconstructed as a procedure. It is not written or
> endorsed by the person named, and any verdict it produces holds only under this
> framework.

## What this lens optimizes for

Calibrating how aggressive to be, given where sentiment and pricing sit in the
cycle. It deliberately refuses to forecast: it takes the temperature of the present
and adjusts posture. Its unit of analysis is the environment, not the company.

## Market-fit note

- The sentiment indicators below are market-structure dependent. In A-shares, use
  margin-financing balances, new account openings, turnover velocity and new-fund
  issuance; in HK, use southbound flows, IPO pipeline activity and the discount
  behaviour of dual-listed pairs; in crypto, funding rates, perpetual open interest
  and leverage liquidations.
- The lens travels well across markets precisely because it measures behaviour, and
  behaviour rhymes even when instruments differ.

## Priority signals (walk in this order)

1. **Second-level formulation.** Restate the question from "is this a good company
   or a good economy?" to "what is the consensus, is it embedded in the price, and
   in what specific way am I differing from it?" If your view and the consensus
   coincide, there is no edge regardless of correctness.
2. **Take the temperature with observables, not opinions.**
   - risk premia: credit spreads versus their own history, equity risk premium
   - issuance quality: covenant terms, IPO quality, ease of financing for weak
     borrowers — the single most reliable indicator here
   - leverage in the system: margin balances, prime brokerage leverage, derivative
     open interest
   - investor behaviour: fund flows, new-account formation, turnover velocity
   - narrative: whether "this time is different" is being said, and by whom
3. **Place the position on the pendulum**, not on a forecast: euphoria → optimism →
   neutral → pessimism → capitulation. State which observations put it there.
4. **Ask what price implies.** Reverse-engineer the growth, default or margin
   assumptions embedded in the current price. A price is a statement of expectation;
   read it before disputing it.
5. **Set posture, not prediction.** The output is a dial — more aggressive, neutral,
   more defensive — with position sizing and quality preferences attached. Never a
   date, never a level.
6. **Asymmetry check.** In this environment, does being wrong cost much more or much
   less than being right pays? Late-cycle, the asymmetry turns hostile long before
   the cycle turns.

## Disqualifiers (hard vetoes)

- **Any output containing a market-timing call** — a level or a date. That is
  outside the lens by construction.
- **Temperature taken from narrative alone**, with no measurable risk-premium,
  issuance or leverage evidence.
- **Contrarianism for its own sake.** Being opposite the crowd is not an edge;
  being opposite the crowd *and* right about a specific mispricing is. State the
  mispricing.
- **Cycle position asserted without acknowledging that extremes persist.** A cycle
  read is not a trigger; if it is being used as one, the lens is being misused.

## Scoring rubric

| Reading | Evidence pattern | Posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Euphoria | Spreads at cycle tights, covenant-lite issuance easy, leverage rising, "new paradigm" talk | Maximum defence: quality up, leverage down, liquidity up |
| Optimism | Premia below average, issuance healthy | Modestly defensive |
| Neutral | Premia near long-run average | Normal posture; the lens adds nothing here, say so |
| Pessimism | Premia above average, issuance selective | Modestly aggressive |
| Capitulation | Forced selling, financing unavailable at any price, quality sold with junk | Maximum aggression the mandate allows |

## Typical misuse

- **Used as a timing tool.** Extremes can persist far longer than a position can be
  financed. The lens says how to be positioned, not when it turns.
- **Applied to a single stock.** A company's fundamentals are not a cycle read;
  conflating them produces confident nonsense in both directions.
- **Sentiment measured with sentiment surveys only.** Surveys are noisy and often
  contradict positioning data. Prefer what people *did* with money — issuance,
  leverage, flows — over what they said.
- **Permanent bearishness laundered as discipline.** Track whether the lens has
  produced any "be aggressive" reading in the last several years; if not, it is
  being applied with a thumb on the scale.
- **Reading a single indicator.** Credit spreads alone, or flows alone, generate
  false extremes. The reading requires convergence across the observable families.

## Falsifiers

- Risk premia widen materially while your reading was euphoria → the temperature
  read was stale; the pendulum had already moved.
- Weak borrowers lose financing access → late-cycle read confirmed regardless of
  index level.
- The consensus you identified turns out not to be the consensus (positioning data
  contradicts the narrative) → the second-level premise was wrong, and the whole
  run is void.

## Output contract

Report under the skill-level output contract. State the verdict as "under a
cycle-positioning framework, …", give the pendulum reading with the observable
evidence per family, and express the conclusion as posture — never as a level or a
date. This is analysis, not investment advice.
