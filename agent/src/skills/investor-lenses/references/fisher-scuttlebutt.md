# Scuttlebutt Growth Lens

> **Framework, not investment advice.** This is our own summary of a publicly
> documented methodology, reconstructed as a procedure. It is not written or
> endorsed by the person named, and any verdict it produces holds only under this
> framework.

## What this lens optimizes for

Finding businesses whose growth comes from a *repeatable organizational capability*
— research productivity, sales organization, cost discipline, customer intimacy —
rather than from one lucky product or one cycle. Its distinguishing move is that
the evidence is gathered from people around the company, not from the company.

## Market-fit note

- Channel evidence in A-shares is frequently available and frequently unreliable:
  distributor commentary is affected by channel-stuffing incentives near
  period-ends. Weight end-customer and ex-employee evidence above distributor
  evidence.
- In HK-listed China operations, on-the-ground evidence and reported segment data
  can diverge for long periods; treat a persistent divergence as a finding, not
  noise.
- For businesses with no reachable ecosystem (holding companies, opaque B2B
  intermediaries), this lens simply cannot be run. Say so rather than substituting
  desk research and calling it scuttlebutt.

## Priority signals (walk in this order)

1. **Runway.** Is there a market large enough for the current products to sustain
   several years of substantial revenue growth? If the growth is already a large
   share of the addressable market, the rest of the lens is moot.
2. **What comes after the current product.** Is there a determination and a
   mechanism to keep developing the next products once the current line matures?
   Look for a pipeline with dates, not aspirations.
3. **Research productivity, not research spending.** R&D as a share of revenue is
   an input. Ask for the output: revenue from products launched in the last three
   years, hit rate on development programs, time from program start to revenue.
4. **Sales organization quality.** Is distribution a genuine capability — coverage,
   attach rates, retention of key accounts, sales productivity per head — or a
   commission scheme? This is the most commonly under-examined asset.
5. **Margin structure and its defence.** Is the margin adequate, and what
   specifically is being done to hold it as costs rise? "Operating leverage" is not
   an answer; pricing, mix, and cost programs are.
6. **Labour and executive relations.** Turnover in the engineering and sales
   organizations, whether promotions come from inside, whether the layer below the
   CEO is visible and credible. Depth of management is a growth constraint.
7. **Cost accounting and controls.** Does management know the profitability of each
   product line and customer, or only the consolidated number? A company that cannot
   answer this cannot allocate capital well.
8. **Candour under bad news.** How did management communicate the last failure —
   the missed launch, the recall, the lost contract? Behaviour in the bad quarter
   predicts behaviour in the next bad quarter.
9. **The scuttlebutt cross-check.** Only after the above: what do customers,
   suppliers, distributors, ex-employees and competitors say about all of it? Ask
   competitors what they fear; ask customers what they would switch to and why they
   have not.

## Disqualifiers (hard vetoes)

- **No independent evidence obtainable.** If every input traces back to company
  materials, the lens has not been run — record it as not-applicable rather than
  passing it.
- **Primary evidence contradicts reported figures** on a material line (channel
  inventory, real end-demand, actual pricing) and the gap is unexplained.
- **Integrity question in management** — this lens explicitly refuses to proceed on
  a business whose management it does not trust, regardless of growth.
- **One-product company with no successor program**, where the current product's
  runway is the entire thesis.
- **Growth bought, not built** — revenue growth driven by acquisitions or by
  extending customer credit, with organic growth flat.

## Scoring rubric

Score each of the nine signals as *strong / adequate / weak / unknown*. The verdict
depends on the *shape*, not the total:

- Weak on runway or successor products → no, whatever the rest looks like.
- Weak on cost accounting or candour → risk-flag, stack the inversion lens.
- Three or more *unknown* → the lens has insufficient evidence; report that, do not
  interpolate.

## Typical misuse

- **Anecdote laundering.** Two enthusiastic customers become "channel checks
  confirm strong demand". Record sample size, who was asked, and what the
  counter-evidence was.
- **Selection bias in sourcing.** Talking only to the customers management
  introduced you to. Note who selected the sample.
- **Qualitative conclusions used to override arithmetic.** Scuttlebutt tells you
  whether the growth is real; it does not tell you what the growth is worth. Price
  discipline must come from another lens.
- **Applied to a cyclical upswing.** Everyone in the ecosystem is optimistic at the
  top of a cycle. Date every piece of qualitative evidence against the cycle.
- **Treating employee-review sites and forums as primary evidence.** Self-selected,
  timing-skewed, and frequently gamed.
- **Compliance blindness.** Pursuing non-public specifics from insiders is not
  research. Stay with observable, public and permissibly shared information.

## Falsifiers

- Revenue from products launched in the last three years falls toward zero →
  research productivity claim fails, and the lens's central premise with it.
- Key-account retention drops while headline revenue grows → growth is coming from
  new-logo churn, not a durable capability.
- The named successor product slips two consecutive periods → the pipeline was
  aspiration.

## Output contract

Report under the skill-level output contract. State the verdict as "under a
scuttlebutt-growth framework, …", and for every qualitative claim record the source
type, sample size and date. This is analysis, not investment advice.
