# Forensic Short Lens

> **Framework, not investment advice.** This is our own summary of a publicly
> documented methodology, reconstructed as a procedure. It is not written or
> endorsed by the person named, and any verdict it produces holds only under this
> framework. It describes an analytical procedure; it is not a recommendation to
> take any position, and short exposure carries unbounded loss.

## What this lens optimizes for

Detecting the gap between what a business reports and what it actually earns in
cash, and separating "expensive" from "impaired". Its most useful output is often
applied to a *long* candidate as a quality screen, not to short anything.

## Market-fit note

- Depends on filing quality. US filings support the full procedure; A-share and HK
  disclosure supports much of it but with fewer segment and related-party details,
  and audit-quality dispersion is wider.
- Structural constraints matter and are part of the analysis, not an afterthought:
  in A-shares securities lending is limited and mechanically constrained; in HK
  borrow exists but can be recalled; single-name shorting may be restricted or
  impossible. A thesis that cannot be expressed is still useful as a *warning*, and
  must be labelled as such.

## Priority signals (walk in this order)

1. **Cash versus accrual divergence.** Cumulative operating cash flow versus
   cumulative net income over three to five years. Persistent divergence is the
   single highest-yield signal in this lens.
2. **Where the divergence lives.** Receivable days, inventory days, payable days,
   deferred and unbilled revenue, capitalized costs (development, customer
   acquisition, interest), and changes in useful-life or depreciation assumptions.
   Name the line item.
3. **Revenue recognition mechanics.** Gross versus net presentation, timing of
   recognition, channel versus end-customer sell-through, bill-and-hold, vendor
   financing of customers, and any revenue that depends on a related party.
4. **Structural impairment of the business model.** Is the end market being
   substituted away? Are unit economics negative at scale and only survivable while
   external capital is cheap? Is the business regulated into a shrinking box?
5. **Serial acquisition as an earnings engine.** Roll-ups that grow reported
   earnings through purchase accounting, restructuring charges treated as
   non-recurring every year, and organic growth that is never disclosed separately.
6. **Financing dependence.** Does the business require continuous external capital?
   Map the maturity wall, covenant headroom, and what happens to the model if
   financing cost rises by several hundred basis points.
7. **Incentives and governance.** Compensation tied to non-GAAP measures the
   company itself defines, auditor changes, CFO turnover, late filings, material
   weaknesses, unusual related-party dealings.
8. **Only now: valuation.** Expensive is not a thesis. Valuation determines the
   size of the potential gap, never its existence.
9. **Expressibility and crowding.** Borrow availability and cost, days-to-cover,
   short interest as a share of float, index and passive ownership, and whether the
   company can issue equity into strength. A correct thesis in a crowded, costly,
   recallable position can still lose entirely.

## Disqualifiers (hard vetoes)

- **"It's overvalued" as the entire case.** Multiple compression alone is not a
  forensic thesis and has no timing anchor.
- **No identified mechanism** — a bad smell with no specific line item, no specific
  policy choice, and no specific cash-versus-accrual gap.
- **The catalyst is unbounded in time** while the cost of carry is real: borrow
  cost, dividends, and the possibility of recall.
- **Unbounded upside risk with no defined exit** — the loss profile is asymmetric
  against the holder, and this lens treats an undefined exit as a veto.
- **Position not expressible in the relevant market** — must be re-labelled as a
  long-side warning rather than a short thesis.

## Scoring rubric

| Layer | Question | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accounting | Is reported profit backed by cash? | Necessary |
| Structural | Is the business model impaired, not just mature? | Necessary |
| Financing | Does it need capital it may not get? | Strengthening |
| Governance | Do incentives reward the distortion? | Strengthening |
| Valuation | How large is the gap if it closes? | Sizing only |
| Expressibility | Can the view be held long enough to be right? | Gate |

Both "necessary" layers must be present. Valuation never substitutes for either.

## Typical misuse

- **Applied to a merely mature business.** Slow growth is not impairment; the lens
  will manufacture a thesis out of ordinary decline if allowed.
- **Accounting conservatism read as fraud.** Aggressive is not fraudulent, and
  policy differences across jurisdictions produce false positives.
- **Ignoring the borrow and the crowd.** Being right after being forced out is
  indistinguishable from being wrong.
- **Confirmation cascade.** Once a short thesis is formed, every data point reads as
  supporting it. Require a written falsifier list before any further evidence is
  gathered.
- **Used on a long book without translation.** As a long-side screen it should
  produce "avoid" or "reduce confidence", not a mirror-image conviction.
- **Publishing the thesis as fact.** Forensic findings are hypotheses about
  accounting choices; state them as such, with the exact filing reference.

## Falsifiers

- Operating cash flow converges to net income over the following year with no
  change in accounting policy → the central divergence signal retires.
- The receivable or inventory build reverses into cash without a write-down → the
  quality concern was a working-capital timing effect.
- The company funds itself through a full cycle without dilution or covenant relief
  → the financing-dependence layer fails.

## Output contract

Report under the skill-level output contract. State the verdict as "under a
forensic-short framework, …", cite the specific line item and filing period for
every accounting claim, keep valuation strictly in the sizing section, and state
explicitly whether the view is expressible or is a long-side warning only. This is
analysis, not investment advice.
