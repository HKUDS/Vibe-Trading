# Debt Cycle Lens

> **Framework, not investment advice.** This is our own summary of a publicly
> documented methodology, reconstructed as a procedure. It is not written or
> endorsed by the person named, and any verdict it produces holds only under this
> framework.

## What this lens optimizes for

Portfolio survivability across regimes. It treats the economy as a mechanism driven
by credit creation and policy response, asks which regime that mechanism is
currently in, and then builds exposure so that no single regime outcome dominates
the result. It is an allocation lens, not a selection lens.

## Market-fit note

- Requires country-level data: debt-to-GDP, debt service burden, policy rate versus
  nominal growth, currency reserves, external debt share, capacity utilization.
- For China, the credit impulse, local-government financing behaviour, property
  sector credit and the state's policy reaction function are the operative
  variables; the "central bank raises rates until it breaks" template of developed
  markets translates poorly.
- For dollarized or reserve-currency economies, the currency and reserve-status
  questions behave differently from those for economies with external debt.

## Priority signals (walk in this order)

1. **Short-term cycle position.** Output gap and capacity utilization, unemployment
   versus its structural level, inflation direction, real policy rate versus real
   growth, credit growth versus nominal GDP growth. Place the economy in: early
   expansion / late expansion / tightening / contraction / reflation.
2. **Long-term debt cycle position.** Total debt to GDP and its trajectory, debt
   service as a share of income, whether debt growth still produces proportional
   output growth, and how much conventional policy room remains (distance to the
   lower bound; central bank balance sheet size).
3. **Which deleveraging path is running**, if any: austerity, default and
   restructuring, monetization, or wealth transfer. Each has a different signature
   for currency, bonds and real assets, and they usually run together in a mix —
   identify the mix, not a single label.
4. **Derive asset-class implications from the regime, mechanically.** Rising growth
   versus expectations favours equities and credit; rising inflation versus
   expectations favours inflation-linked bonds and commodities; falling growth
   favours nominal bonds; monetized deleveraging pressures the currency and favours
   real assets. Write the mapping explicitly instead of jumping to a conclusion.
5. **Currency as the release valve.** In a debt problem denominated in the home
   currency, the currency usually absorbs the adjustment. Ask what the local-currency
   result and the hard-currency result each look like — they can differ in sign.
6. **Build from uncorrelated bets.** Count the number of return streams whose
   drivers are genuinely different, not the number of line items. Correlations
   measured in calm periods understate what happens in stress; test the pairwise
   relationships in the last two crisis windows, not the full-sample average.
7. **Balance across regimes.** State how the portfolio performs in each of the four
   growth/inflation quadrants. If one quadrant is catastrophic, the portfolio
   embodies a forecast, whether or not one was intended.

## Disqualifiers (hard vetoes)

- **A regime call with no mechanism.** "Inflation is coming" without the credit,
  capacity and policy chain that produces it is a prediction, not a lens output.
- **Diversification counted by instrument rather than by driver.** Ten equity
  exposures across sectors and geographies are close to one bet in a stress window.
- **A portfolio with a catastrophic quadrant** presented as balanced.
- **Correlation assumptions taken from a calm-period full-sample average** with no
  stress-window check.
- **Applied to pick a single stock.** Out of scope. The lens has nothing to say
  about company-level competitive position.

## Scoring rubric

| Output | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Short-cycle placement | Named phase + the five indicators that place it |
| Long-cycle placement | Debt/GDP trajectory, debt service burden, policy room remaining |
| Deleveraging mix | Which of the four paths, in what proportion |
| Asset-class map | Explicit regime → asset mapping, written before the conclusion |
| Diversification count | Number of distinct *drivers*, with stress-window correlations |
| Quadrant table | Portfolio outcome in each growth/inflation quadrant |

## Typical misuse

- **Template applied without local translation.** The developed-market credit cycle
  template misreads economies where credit allocation is administratively directed
  and policy reacts on a different function.
- **Regime read converted into a market-timing trade.** Regimes are identified late
  and change slowly; the appropriate output is exposure shape, not entry timing.
- **Historical analogy as evidence.** "This looks like 1937" is a hypothesis
  generator. The mechanism has to be re-derived from current data, or the analogy is
  doing the work.
- **Risk-parity mechanics assumed to be the lens.** Leveraging low-volatility assets
  is one implementation with its own failure mode — a correlated rise in real rates
  hurts every leg simultaneously. The lens is the regime analysis, not the leverage
  recipe.
- **Confusing macro correctness with profit.** Being right about the regime and
  wrong about what is already priced is a loss. Stack a cycle-positioning lens to
  check what the market has already embedded.

## Falsifiers

- Credit growth accelerates while debt service burden falls → the late-long-cycle
  read is wrong; policy room is larger than assumed.
- Two of the "uncorrelated" return streams move together through a stress window →
  the diversification count was fictional and the portfolio shape must be rebuilt.
- Currency strengthens through a monetized deleveraging → the release-valve
  assumption fails and the hard-currency mapping is void.
